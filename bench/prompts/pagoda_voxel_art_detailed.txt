ART DIRECTION

Use a rich but coherent palette: vermilion wood, dark teal roof tiles, warm stone, golden ornaments, several greens, pink and white blossoms, and turquoise water.

Use visible cubic or stepped geometry throughout. Avoid a scene made from a few oversized cubes, flat platforms, and identical trees. Small features should remain readable at the initial camera distance.

PAGODA
- Five clearly separated, progressively smaller floors.
- Broad stepped roofs with visibly upturned corners, layered eaves, ridge ornaments, and a golden finial.
- Red columns, cream wall panels, dark wooden beams, window lattices, balconies, and railings.
- A raised stone foundation, an entrance staircase, and warm lanterns at the doorway.
- Give the viewer useful details on the sides and back as well as the front.

3. A small waterfall descending from an elevated rocky stream into the pond.
4. A winding stepping-stone path connecting the entrance gate, bridge, pagoda, and a quiet seating area.

9. Several terrain elevations, retaining walls, and steps. Integrate objects into the terrain rather than placing everything on one flat slab.

Make a garden that feels designed and inhabited. Leave breathing room around the pagoda and preserve clear views of the bridge and pond. Avoid evenly spacing every object or hiding the central architecture behind trees.
